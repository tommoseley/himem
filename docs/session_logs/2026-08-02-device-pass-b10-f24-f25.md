## B10 — THE HEADLINE. Not our bug, and it cost most of a pass.

**Symptom:** iPhone voice recordings were silent. Files written at exactly the right size, correct duration, correct frame counts — and no audio. Camera also dead. iPad fine.

**Resolution: Xcode Device Hub was holding the device's media services.** Disconnecting the phone from Device Hub restored capture.

**The reproduction, which is the part worth keeping:**

> reboot → **Voice Memos works** → open HiMem (attached to Device Hub) → **Voice Memos AND Camera are now both dead**, and stay dead until reboot.

Cross-app breakage is the tell. Once another app is also silent, nothing in our code can be responsible.

### Why it took so long, stated plainly

A held device leaves the app's own state **perfectly plausible at every layer we log**: session activates, engine starts, `isRecording == true`, tap fires with correct frame counts, file written at exactly the right size for its duration. The only difference is that the samples are zeros. Every instrument agreed the recording was fine.

**It also survives a bisect.** Building `4a08423^` (pre-F18) to the iPhone produced identical `in_peak = 0.0000`. That correctly cleared our diff — but "not our code" then reads as "must be the hardware", skipping the actual answer. **After "not our code", the next suspect is the measuring apparatus, before the device.**

Two wrong turns are recorded honestly because they are the reason for two of the new rules:

1. **An audio-route / Bluetooth theory** was raised while the causing change sat two days deep in the diff. Tom's correction: *"this WORKED, 100%, before the troika changes — this is something we just broke."* → the Suspect-Your-Own-Diff rule.
2. **The compressor was exonerated using a transcription of a file recorded on an EARLIER build.** Evidence that a component "worked" must come from the current build on the current path.

### THE PROTOCOL — the takeaway

> **For anything touching capture: install, DISCONNECT, then test.** A reading taken while attached measures the attachment.

Recorded in `CLAUDE.md` (`1d169e8`). Tells: **exact** zeros (gain problems attenuate to ~0.01; a held device gives `0.0000` indefinitely) · cross-app breakage · survives a bisect.

## Still open

### 1 · The `in_peak == 0` capture gate — ruled, designed, NOT BUILT

A recording that lands at peak 0.0000 is a capture failure. Today the app saves it, compresses it, transcribes it to nothing, and says nothing — the silent-success class, in the one place where it costs a **memory** rather than a tap.

**Ruled copy:** *"We didn't hear anything. Check that HiMem can use the microphone, and try again."*
**Placement:** where the saved-clip confirmation would have been.
**The recording is ALWAYS KEPT** — we never discard audio on our own judgment that it is empty. The user decides.

**Debugger-suppression design, in full (recommended, not yet ruled):**

- Suppress the **user-facing banner** when a debugger is attached — otherwise it fires constantly under Device Hub and we learn to ignore it within a day, and then it is dead when it matters.
- Gate on **`P_TRACED` (debugger attached), NOT `#if DEBUG`** — so an untethered TestFlight build still shows it. That is the case that matters for Judi.
- **Never suppress the detection or the `[Amp]` log.**
- **Announce the suppression** so it is not a silent skip: `[HiMem][Speech][Amp] capture was silent — banner suppressed (debugger attached)`. Nothing is hidden; the distinction from a forbidden opt-out is that detection still runs and still speaks.
